## What Is the Manelli Method? {#what-is}

The **Manelli Method** is a signature foil highlight technique built around a **double zigzag parting** — one zigzag at the top of the section, one at the bottom — combined with a **Dream Weaver comb** to separate the mids and ends of the hair before the foil goes in.

The result is what I call a **broken zigzag**: an irregular, diffused line of color at the root that mimics the look of teasy lights without any actual teasing. The color melts into the natural hair with a soft, lived-in root that doesn't need a shadow root to look intentional. It grows out beautifully. It saves time in the chair. And it works on nearly every client who walks through your door.

That is the whole technique in one paragraph. The rest of this post is about understanding *why* it works so you can execute it with confidence — not just copy the mechanics.

## Why I Created It: The Problem With Teasing {#why-i-created}

Teasy lights became a trend because clients loved the result. A soft, dimensional highlight that looked like the sun had kissed your hair — not a line drawn across your scalp. The problem was never the result. The problem was the process.

**Teasing is unpredictable.** Two stylists with the same level of training will tease a section differently every single time. The amount of backcombing, the depth of the tease, how much hair gets caught up in the process — all of it varies, which means the end result varies too. Even the same stylist will tease a section differently at 9am versus 5pm when their hands are tired.

**Teasing is time-consuming.** A full head of traditional teasy lights takes significantly longer than a standard foil service. You're adding a manipulation step to every single section. On a full highlight client, that adds up fast.

**Teasing is hard on the hair.** Backcombing creates mechanical damage. For clients with fine hair, fragile ends, or already-compromised strands from previous color services, teasing adds stress to hair that can't afford it.

I started thinking about what, exactly, the tease was accomplishing. The answer was simple: it was breaking up the clean line where the foil started. It was creating irregular color uptake at the root so the highlight didn't have a hard edge. It was mimicking natural, grown-in color.

Once I understood the *function* of the tease, I could figure out how to accomplish that function without the tease itself. That's where the double zigzag came from.

## How It's Different From a Standard Teasy Light {#how-its-different}

In a traditional teasy light, the parting is usually straight or a single simple zigzag, and the diffusion at the root comes entirely from the backcombing. The color goes on a section that has been mechanically disrupted.

In the Manelli Method, the diffusion comes from the **geometry of the parting itself**. There is no backcombing. No mechanical disruption. The double zigzag creates a naturally irregular boundary between what's in the foil and what isn't — which means the color graduates into the root in an organic, broken way without any additional steps.

Here's the key difference in practice:

| | Traditional Teasy Light | Manelli Method |
|---|---|---|
| Root diffusion created by | Backcombing | Double zigzag geometry |
| Processing steps | Part + tease + foil | Part + Dream Weaver + foil |
| Consistency | Variable (hand-dependent) | Highly consistent |
| Hair stress | Higher (mechanical damage) | Lower |
| Time per section | Longer | Faster |
| Shadow root needed | Often | Rarely |

The Manelli Method doesn't just produce a similar result to teasy lights — for many clients, it produces a *better* result, because the diffusion is more controlled and more consistent from section to section.

## The Double Zigzag: Step-by-Step Breakdown {#step-by-step}

This is the core mechanic. Read this carefully.

[Image: Close-up diagram of double zigzag parting — alt: "double zigzag parting diagram for Manelli Method foil highlights"]

### Step 1: Take your section as normal

Work in your standard horizontal sections, just as you would for any foil service. Section size will depend on how dense you want the result.

### Step 2: Create the top zigzag

At the top of your section, part with a zigzag rather than a straight line. The zigzag doesn't need to be dramatic — a subtle, irregular zigzag is all you need. Think of it as an imperfect line rather than sharp peaks and valleys.

### Step 3: Create the bottom zigzag

Here's what makes the Manelli Method different from a standard zigzag part. At the **bottom** of the same section, create a second zigzag. This bottom parting is what creates the broken base of the highlight — the part that gives you that diffused root without any teasing.

The two zigzags do not need to mirror each other. In fact, they shouldn't. The irregularity between the two is what creates the natural, broken look at the root once the color is processed.

### Step 4: Use the Dream Weaver comb on the mids and ends

Before the foil goes in, use a **Dream Weaver comb** to separate the mids and ends of the section. This is a weaving step — you're picking up strands rather than taking everything. The comb makes this fast, precise, and consistent in a way that hand weaving simply can't match at the same speed.

### Step 5: Apply the foil as normal

Place your foil under the section you've isolated, apply your lightener or color, and fold. From here, the service continues exactly as any standard foil highlight would.

That's it. Five steps, no teasing, and a root that grows out beautifully every single time.

## The Dream Weaver Comb: Why It Matters {#dream-weaver}

The **Dream Weaver comb** is the tool that makes this technique scalable. Without it, the weaving step in the mids and ends would slow you down significantly — you'd be hand-weaving every section, which defeats the time-saving purpose of the whole approach.

The Dream Weaver comb lets you pick up hair in a consistent, controlled way at speed. The result is even color distribution through the mid-lengths and ends that complements the broken root you've created with the double zigzag.

If you don't already have a Dream Weaver comb in your kit, it's worth the investment. It's not just useful for the Manelli Method — it's a versatile tool for any technique that involves weaving rather than slicing.

## When to Use the Manelli Method {#when-to-use}

This technique was built to be versatile. Here are the situations where it performs best:

**Color correction clients.** When a client comes in with banding, spottiness, or the aftermath of a DIY color attempt, the double zigzag naturally diffuses through those problem areas. The irregular parting works *with* existing inconsistencies in the hair rather than against them.

**Brunette-to-blonde transformations.** Clients going from dark natural color to a lighter result benefit enormously from a technique that builds in a natural root. The Manelli Method creates a lived-in look from the very first service.

**Lived-in and low-maintenance clients.** If your client can only get into the salon every four to six months, you need a technique that grows out gracefully. This is it.

**Clients who want to avoid a shadow root.** The broken zigzag creates enough diffusion at the root that a separate shadow root application is usually unnecessary. That's one less step and a faster service.

**Clients with fine or fragile hair.** Because there's no backcombing, the Manelli Method is gentler on the hair shaft — which matters enormously for clients whose hair can't handle additional mechanical stress.

## Why You Don't Need a Shadow Root With This Technique {#no-shadow-root}

A shadow root exists to solve one problem: a highlight with a hard edge at the root looks unnatural. It makes color look "placed" rather than grown-in. The shadow root softens that edge.

The Manelli Method eliminates the hard edge in the parting step itself. Because the top and bottom zigzags create an irregular boundary, there is no clean line for the eye to follow from natural hair into highlighted hair. The transition is already broken up before any product is applied.

This doesn't mean you can never add a shadow root on a Manelli Method client — there are cases where a deeper root is specifically requested as a design element. But it means you're adding it as a creative choice, not as a corrective step. That distinction matters for your process and your pricing.

## What Results to Expect {#results}

When the Manelli Method is executed correctly, here's what you and your client should expect:

- A soft, diffused root line with no hard edges

- A result that reads as lived-in and natural from day one
- A grow-out that looks intentional rather than overdue at the four, six, and even eight-month mark
- Faster service times compared to traditional teasy lights
- Consistent results across different stylists executing the same technique

The Manelli Method is not a one-size-fits-all formula — like any technique, the outcome depends on section size, developer strength, product choice, and starting level. But the core mechanic is repeatable, teachable, and scalable in a way that teasing-dependent techniques simply aren't.

## Frequently Asked Questions {#faq}

**What is the Manelli Method?**
The Manelli Method is a foil highlight technique created by Mirella Manelli that uses a double zigzag parting and a Dream Weaver comb to create soft, lived-in roots without any teasing or backcombing.

**Is the Manelli Method the same as teasy lights?**
No. Teasy lights use backcombing to diffuse the root. The Manelli Method achieves the same visual result through the geometry of a double zigzag parting — no mechanical disruption of the hair required.

**Do I need a shadow root with the Manelli Method?**
Usually not. The double zigzag creates natural diffusion at the root that eliminates the need for a separate shadow root application in most cases.

**What comb do I need for the Manelli Method?**
A Dream Weaver comb is used to separate the mids and ends before foiling. It makes the technique fast and consistent.

**What clients is the Manelli Method best for?**
It works particularly well for color correction clients, brunette-to-blonde transformations, lived-in and low-maintenance clients, and anyone with fine or fragile hair who shouldn't be exposed to additional mechanical damage.
