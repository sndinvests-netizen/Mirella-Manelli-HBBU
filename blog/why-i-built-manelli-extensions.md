# Why I Stopped Using Every Other Extension Brand (And Built My Own)

*By Mirella Manelli*

---

I want to be honest with you about something.

Before I launched Manelli, I spent years recommending extension brands to my clients with a smile on my face — while quietly dreading the follow-up appointments.

The shedding. The tangling. The client who came back six weeks after a full install looking like she'd been through a wind tunnel. I would sit across from her, assess the damage, and think: *this is not my work. This is the product.*

After twenty years behind the chair, I know the difference.

---

## The Problem Nobody Was Talking About

Here's what most extension brands don't tell you: a lot of what goes wrong has nothing to do with the stylist's technique. It's the weft itself.

The conventional machine weft — the workhorse of the extension industry for decades — has a structural problem. The hair strands are stitched together in a way that looks great in the package but starts to break down the moment it's installed and lived in. Wash it a few times. Sleep on it. Run a brush through it. The stitching loosens, the strands migrate, and you end up with what we call the **mushroom effect**: a thick, bunched-up ridge at the base of the weft that's impossible to hide and uncomfortable to wear.

I watched it happen over and over again. I'd do beautiful work on a client — perfect placement, seamless blend, color-matched within a shade — and then the product would undercut everything.

And the hand-tied weft, while elegant, has its own limitations. It's delicate. You can't cut it to size without unraveling the whole thing. On clients with finer hair or unusual growth patterns, you're working around the weft instead of with it.

I tried brand after brand. I read the marketing. I went to the classes. And I kept coming back to the same conclusion: **the products were designed for shelf appeal, not for what actually happens after the install.**

---

## What a Stylist Actually Needs

When you've been doing this as long as I have, you develop a very clear picture of what a professional-grade extension needs to do:

**It needs to not shed.** Full stop. Shedding isn't a minor inconvenience — it's an erosion of your client's trust. Every strand that ends up in the drain is a text message you're going to get.

**It needs to be customizable.** No two clients have the same nape, the same growth pattern, the same density. A weft that can't be sized to fit the actual person in your chair is a weft that's compromising your results.

**It needs to hold up to real life.** Clients sleep in their extensions. They work out. They swim. They forget to use the right shampoo. A luxury product that only performs under ideal conditions isn't luxury — it's fragile.

**It needs to last.** A quality install should carry a client 12 months or more with proper maintenance. Anything less is just recycled income for the brand at your client's expense.

No brand I was using was hitting all four. Most were hitting one or two and calling it premium.

---

## So I Built What I Couldn't Find

Manelli started the way a lot of things do in this industry — out of pure frustration and a refusal to accept that this is just how it is.

I wanted a hybrid weft: the structural strength of a machine weft combined with the flexibility and comfort of a hand-tied construction. I wanted anti-shed technology that actually worked — not a marketing claim, but a construction method that keeps strands anchored at the weft throughout the product's lifespan. I wanted wefts that could be cut to any size without unraveling, so stylists could customize each install to the individual client sitting in front of them.

And I wanted the hair itself to be right. 100% cuticle-intact Remy human hair, with the cuticles aligned in the same direction. This is what determines how hair behaves — how it moves, how it blends with natural hair, how it responds to heat and color. Misaligned cuticles tangle. Aligned cuticles flow.

It took time to get there. A lot of testing, a lot of failed samples, a lot of installs on clients I trusted to give me honest feedback. But eventually we had something I could stand behind — something I could put my name on — and something that finally matched what I'd been promising clients for two decades.

---

## This Is Personal

I know there are a lot of extension brands out there right now. New ones launch every season. And I understand the skepticism — I had it too, about every brand I tried.

What I can tell you is this: Manelli exists because I was unwilling to keep recommending products I didn't fully believe in. It exists because my clients deserve to leave my chair and still have beautiful hair six months later. And it exists because stylists deserve tools that protect their reputation, not undermine it.

Every decision I made in building this line — the hybrid weft design, the anti-shed construction, the cuttable sizing, the hair sourcing — came from standing behind a chair and knowing exactly what was missing.

Twenty years of frustration turned into one product I'm genuinely proud of.

That's Manelli.
